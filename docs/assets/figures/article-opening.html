  1. This is one function: battle, a small card fight. It asks for dice (Roll), a player (Choose), a screen (Show) and a count (Played). It never says who answers.
  2. Each question is an effect. At yield Choose(...) the function stops and hands the question out, and what it has left to do becomes a value too.
  3. The effect travels out to a handler, and the handler also gets the rest of the function, called k.
  4. scripted answers from its list of moves. It resumes k with the next move, and battle goes on from its yield.
  5. Stack handlers around it. Each effect travels out to the first ring whose handler takes it: Choose to the script, Roll to the dice, Show to the screen, Played to the counter.
  6. A scripted player and a screen give the same fight on every run: three runs, ('win', 6) each time.
  7. Swap in a random bot for the script. It answers Choose with a Roll of its own, which travels on out to the dice.
  8. Take the screen away: silent takes every Show and draws nothing. The function does not change, not one line of battle.
  9. Add a recorder. record passes each effect on out and keeps the answer that comes back: one fight leaves 32 answers on the tape.
  10. replay(tape) answers every Choose and Roll from the tape. With no dice and no player, the whole fight comes out the same.
  11. A Monte Carlo bot answers a question by running the fight itself, while the fight that asked waits as k.
  12. Run a hundred fights at once, each a task with its own dice, under scheduled. The random bot wins 73.
  13. Give the same hundred fights the Monte Carlo bot, and it wins all one hundred.
  14. One function, unchanged, in every stack: a script, a random bot, a recorder, a replay, a Monte Carlo bot and a hundred tasks. That is algebraic effects in Python, with doeff.
